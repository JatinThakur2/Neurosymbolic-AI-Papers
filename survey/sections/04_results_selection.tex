\textbf{Exclusions.} The most frequent reason is E3, no interacting explicit symbolic component (Table~\ref{tab:exclusions}). The query is broad by design, so it retrieves many papers that use ``symbolic'', ``formal'' or ``program'' in a sense the protocol excludes: chain-of-thought prompting described as symbolic reasoning, retrieval of knowledge-graph triples, generic tool use, and code generation judged only by unit tests (rules R3--R6). E1 removes models trained from scratch on synthetic symbolic data and papers whose ``transformer'' is not a language model (R1).

\subsection{Search recall}
\label{sec:selection-recall}
Across the audit strata of the full search, the double-screened audit found \cnt{audtwolmx} eligible studies among \cnt{audtwolmn} sampled records with language-model terms only, \cnt{audtwosymx} among \cnt{audtwosymn} with symbolic terms only, and \cnt{audtwoneix} among \cnt{audtwonein} with neither (screener agreement on the audit records $\kappa=\cnt{kappaaudit}$ and \cnt{kappaauditext} for the extension). Scaled to the strata, about \cnt{missedtwo} eligible Source B studies (95\% interval \cnt{missedtwolo}--\cnt{missedtwohi}) were not retrieved. Search recall is estimated at \cnt{recalltwo} (95\% interval \cnt{recalltwolo}--\cnt{recalltwohi}; the plug-in estimate is \cnt{recalltwoplugin}). For stage~1 alone, the estimate was \cnt{recallmedian} (\cnt{recalllo}--\cnt{recallhi}): the stage-2 query found additional studies, but the extended audit also found more missed ones, so the estimate for the full search did not rise.

The missed studies illustrate why: most of them describe their symbolic component in words the blocks do not contain. They include game and puzzle benchmarks with rule-based generators, agents that act by writing programs, a static-analysis tool driven by a language model, and a vision-language model trained with executable assertions. Six of the eight found in the extension were marked \texttt{unsure} by at least one screener, so they lie near the eligibility boundary rather than in its core. A vocabulary-independent search would have to screen tens of thousands of abstracts; we accept a recall of about one half and read every count in this paper as a count of the \emph{retrievable} literature at these venues. Proportions and associations are unbiased only if missed studies resemble found ones. The \cnt{afn} studies found only by the audit are too few to test this formally, but they resemble the found ones in the shares that matter most here: \cnt{afAsix} are A6 benchmarks (\cnt{allAsixpct}\% of all included studies are A6) and \cnt{afFthree} are program-aided (\cnt{allFthreepct}\% overall).

\textbf{Known-item check.} Of the \cnt{kiref} references of the independent survey~\cite{cheng2025empowering} at our venues, \cnt{kisrcb} are in Source B; the other \cnt{kinotsrcb} appeared in ACL Findings volumes, which are out of scope. \cnt{kielig} of the references are eligible under our protocol, and the search found \cnt{kifound} of them (\cnt{kifoundpct}\%, 95\% CI \cnt{kifoundlo}--\cnt{kifoundhi}); the one it missed, a logic-guided data-augmentation method, names its model only as ``RoBERTa'', which the language-model block does not match; it is now included. Known-item recall is higher than the audit estimate because the survey covers logical reasoning, where abstracts name their solvers and provers, whereas the audit's misses come from benchmarks and agents described in other terms. Nine landmark studies that the pilot version missed (PAL~\cite{gao2023pal}, SatLM~\cite{ye2023satlm}, Binder~\cite{cheng2023binding}, Draft-Sketch-Prove~\cite{jiang2023draft}, LeanDojo~\cite{yang2023leandojo}, Thor~\cite{jiang2022thor}, autoformalisation with LLMs~\cite{wu2022autoformalization}, LLM-SR~\cite{shojaee2025sr} and Lean-STaR~\cite{lin2025lean}) are all included, but because the protocol was revised after they were known, they are not independent evidence of recall.
