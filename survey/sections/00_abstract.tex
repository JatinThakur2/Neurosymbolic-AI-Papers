\begin{abstract}
Language models now write the programs, proofs and formal specifications that symbolic engines execute or check, but how this division of labour is organised has not been mapped systematically. We report a scoping review, following PRISMA-ScR, of \cnt{included} studies from ten AI and NLP venues (2020--2026) in which a language model---from pretrained sequence models to LLMs---interacts with an explicit symbolic component. The dominant design places a symbolic checker in the loop (\cnt{A2pct}\% of studies), yet most of these checks are empirical---tests, execution, data fit---rather than sound (\cnt{A2empirical} of \cnt{A2}), mostly in program-aided designs. Adjusting for venue mix, the odds that a study uses a checker in the loop rose by a factor of \cnt{trAtwo} per year (95\% CI \cnt{trAtwolo}--\cnt{trAtwohi}), while symbolic guidance without a verdict declined; both trends hold under three alternative orders of our coding scheme. Normalised by accepted papers, the area grew about \cnt{rr}-fold between 2020 and 2025 (95\% CI \cnt{rrlo}--\cnt{rrhi}); relative to all language-model papers its share changed little between 2021--22 and 2025 (rate ratio \cnt{rrttlm}, 95\% CI \cnt{rrttlmlo}--\cnt{rrttlmhi}), so the growth largely tracks that of language-model research. Only \cnt{nesytitlepct}\% of studies call themselves neurosymbolic. Evaluation is fragmented: \cnt{benchsinglepct}\% of the benchmarks named in abstracts appear in a single study, and the most shared one, miniF2F, is near saturation. Studies come from an abstract-level search of \cnt{sourceb} accepted papers, a curated corpus, snowballing and a fresh known-item check; a double-screened stratified audit estimates search recall at \cnt{recalltwo} (95\% interval \cnt{recalltwolo}--\cnt{recalltwohi}), so the map covers roughly half of the eligible literature at these venues. Screening and coding were done by independent LLM reviewers with adjudication.

\end{abstract}
