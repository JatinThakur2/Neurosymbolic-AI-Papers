\textbf{Growth, and what it is relative to.} The pilot reported growth as the share of a title-keyword corpus, which would also rise if recent authors were simply more likely to call their work ``neurosymbolic''. Three measures address this (Fig.~\ref{fig:trend}).

First, normalised by all accepted papers at venue-years with constant coverage, included studies in the five-venue panel (ICLR, ICML, NeurIPS, ACL, EMNLP) rise from \cnt{pfive2020} per 1{,}000 papers in 2020 to \cnt{pfive2023} in 2023 and \cnt{pfive2025} in 2025, a rate ratio of \cnt{rr} (exact 95\% CI \cnt{rrlo}--\cnt{rrhi}). The 2020 rate rests on only \cnt{rrkone} studies, hence the wide interval; against 2021--2022 the 2025 rate is \cnt{rrtt} times higher (\cnt{rrttlo}--\cnt{rrtthi}).

Second, normalised by accepted papers that match the language-model block, the picture changes. The share of all accepted papers that are about language models rose from \cnt{lmshare2020}\% in 2020 to \cnt{lmshare2025}\% in 2025, and neurosymbolic integration grew with it: \cnt{pfivelm2020} included studies per 1{,}000 language-model papers in 2020, \cnt{pfivelm2021} in 2021 and \cnt{pfivelm2025} in 2025. Between 2021--2022 and 2025 the rate ratio is \cnt{rrttlm} (\cnt{rrttlmlo}--\cnt{rrttlmhi}). In this panel, integrating symbolic components has held a roughly stable share of language-model research, so the area's growth until 2025 largely is the growth of language-model research. The ICLR+ICML panel, the only one covering 2026, suggests the share may now be rising (rate ratio 2026 versus 2021--2022 \cnt{rrtwlm}, \cnt{rrtwlmlo}--\cnt{rrtwlmhi}).

Third, inclusion does not depend on the term: only \cnt{nesytitle} included studies (\cnt{nesytitlepct}\%) use ``neurosymbolic'' or ``neural-symbolic'' in the title, and \cnt{nesyany} (\cnt{nesyanypct}\%) anywhere in title or abstract. Because our language-model block also contains vocabulary that became common only after 2022, later years could be easier to retrieve. Counting only studies whose abstracts use the language-model vocabulary already common before 2022 (which \cnt{drifttwentysix}\% of 2026 Source B inclusions still do) gives a 2020--2025 rate ratio of \cnt{rrst} (\cnt{rrstlo}--\cnt{rrsthi}), essentially unchanged. Recall may still differ by year for other reasons; the audit is too small to estimate it per year. The 2026 figures cover ICLR and ICML (and the 2026 records of the curated corpus); the other 2026 proceedings were not in Source B.

\textbf{From guidance to checking, adjusted for venue mix.} Across all venues, checker-in-the-loop designs (A2) rise from \cnt{earlyAtwo}\% of studies in 2020--2022 [95\% CI \cnt{earlyAtwolo}--\cnt{earlyAtwohi}] to \cnt{ysixAtwo}\% in 2026 [\cnt{ysixAtwolo}--\cnt{ysixAtwohi}], and symbolic guidance (A3) falls from \cnt{earlyAthree}\% to \cnt{ysixAthree}\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:archperiod}). This comparison is confounded by venue mix: the 2026 studies come almost entirely from ICLR and ICML, while A3 is common at the NLP venues (\cnt{athreenlp} of the \cnt{athreeall} A3 studies), which contribute no 2026 studies. Two analyses remove the confound.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Constant-venue panel.} On ICLR+ICML alone, A2 accounts for \cnt{ptwythreeAtwo}\% [\cnt{ptwythreeAtwolo}--\cnt{ptwythreeAtwohi}] of studies in 2023 and \cnt{ptwysixAtwo}\% [\cnt{ptwysixAtwolo}--\cnt{ptwysixAtwohi}] in 2026; A3 changes from \cnt{ptwythreeAthree}\% to \cnt{ptwysixAthree}\%. The panel has only \cnt{ptwearlyn} studies in 2020--2022, too few for that period to carry weight.
  \item \emph{Venue fixed effects.} A logistic regression on publication year with venue fixed effects, using all studies, gives an odds ratio per year of \cnt{trAtwo} (95\% CI \cnt{trAtwolo}--\cnt{trAtwohi}) for A2, \cnt{trAthree} (\cnt{trAthreelo}--\cnt{trAthreehi}) for A3 and \cnt{trAone} (\cnt{trAonelo}--\cnt{trAonehi}) for A1. On method papers only the A2 odds ratio is \cnt{trmAtwo} (\cnt{trmAtwolo}--\cnt{trmAtwohi}).
\end{itemize}
Within venues, then, checking designs gain and guidance designs lose ground, and the result holds under every decision-tree order we tried (Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy-sens}). The size of the shift is smaller than the all-venue comparison suggests, because part of that comparison reflects which venues have published in 2026. Translate-and-solve designs (A1) show no consistent trend within venues (odds ratio \cnt{trAone}); on the ICLR+ICML panel their share fluctuates between 2023 and 2025 before falling in 2026, so a recent decline cannot be ruled out.

\textbf{Exact checking at inference.} Exact checks on benchmark answers (A6) and exact checks at inference (A2) are different things, so we report them separately. Among method papers, the share using an exact checker in the loop rises from \cnt{pallearlyinfexact}\% in 2020--2022 to \cnt{pallysixinfexact}\% in 2026, with a venue-adjusted odds ratio of \cnt{trmexact} per year (\cnt{trmexactlo}--\cnt{trmexacthi}), driven by formal theorem proving and verified code generation. Benchmarks with an exact answer checker grow alongside, from \cnt{benchexactearly} to \cnt{benchexactsix} studies.

Checking designs need a model capable of emitting well-formed formal artifacts, which is consistent with their timing.

\textbf{Two communities that rarely meet.} Probabilistic logic programming, abductive learning, inductive logic programming and reasoning-shortcut analysis are well represented in the curated corpus~\cite{huang2021scallop,maene2024hardness,marconato2023neuro,yang2024analysis}. Yet only \cnt{classicaln} of the \cnt{included} included studies name such semantics or diagnostics in their abstract---probabilistic logic, Scallop, semantic loss, Markov logic, inductive logic programming, tractable probabilistic models, probabilistic circuits or knowledge compilation---for example neurosymbolic losses and tractable probabilistic models for constrained generation~\cite{ahmed2023pseudo,ahmed2025controllable,zhang2023tractable}. By this abstract-level measure, the language-model line has not yet adopted the classical line's tools for calibrated inference and for detecting satisfied-for-the-wrong-reason symbols.
