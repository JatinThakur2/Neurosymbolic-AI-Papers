We thank the referee for a careful report that recomputed our tables and asked the right questions. We made every change the report asks for that can be made with data, and several it suggests as options. In brief:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Trends} are now estimated within venues (logistic regression with venue fixed effects) and on the constant ICLR+ICML panel. The shift from guidance to checking survives, and it survives four decision-tree orders, but it is smaller than the all-venue comparison suggested; we say so (M3, M8).
  \item \textbf{Growth} is reported relative to all papers \emph{and} to language-model papers, with exact intervals and a vocabulary control. Relative to language-model papers the share has been stable since 2021; the abstract now says this instead of a bare ``14-fold'' (M4).
  \item \textbf{Search.} We ran the augmented query the referee proposed (stage~2), completed Source B from the ACL Anthology, double-screened and extended the recall audit, and added a fresh known-item check from an independent survey. The included set grew from 659 to \cnt{included} studies. Recall is now estimated at \cnt{recalltwo} (95\% interval \cnt{recalltwolo}--\cnt{recalltwohi}): narrower, but not higher. We report this plainly (M5, M6).
  \item \textbf{Taxonomy.} A separate, double-coded contribution-type axis and a record of every mechanism each study uses; order-sensitivity and program-aided-study sensitivity analyses (M7, M8).
  \item \textbf{Full-text check} of a random sample of A1/A2 studies. It shows that the exact/empirical split is reliable, that most check-strength disagreements are definitional (A1 execution), and that abstracts under-report faithfulness by a wide margin. The faithfulness finding has been rewritten and removed from the abstract (M9).
  \item \textbf{Human verification}: precision of inclusions is now the headline reliability figure, the main findings are reported on human-confirmed studies, and an independent second human rater provides a human--human baseline (M1, M2).
  \item \textbf{Anonymity}: a single switch produces a double-blind version (M10).
\end{itemize}

\section*{Major issues}

\begin{referee}M1. The human check shows about 20\% false inclusions, and nothing was corrected. [\ldots] (a) adjudicate all inclusions at the E3 boundary; (b) report the key proportions on the human-confirmed subset; (c) state precision in the abstract.\end{referee}
\resp We adopted (b) and (c).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{(c) Precision in the abstract.} The abstract now reports precision of inclusions with its interval \ifhumancheck(\cnt{precision}\%, 95\% CI \cnt{precisionlo}--\cnt{precisionhi})\else\pending{value from the human check}\fi{} instead of $\kappa$. Section~IV (``Human checks'') explains why $\kappa$ over all screened records overstates reliability when exclusions dominate.
  \item \emph{(b) Human-confirmed sensitivity analysis.} Section~V-B reports the architecture mix, the A2 trend, the association and faithfulness reporting on the confirmed inclusions \ifhumancheck(\cnt{hcn} studies)\else\pending{n}\fi. The validity section states that all counts are upper bounds.
  \item \emph{Why the sampled decisions were not corrected one by one (your Question 4).} Correcting only the 30\% that a human happened to see would make the included set depend on which records were sampled, and the corrected counts would mix two error rates. We instead treat precision as a property of the whole map and show how the findings change on confirmed studies. We also report precision separately for inclusions decided by an adjudicator and for those on which both screeners agreed (\S5 of the report).
  \item \emph{(a) Re-adjudicating every E3-boundary inclusion} would be another LLM pass unless done by a human. We did not do it, and say so; if the editor prefers it, the decision files make it a mechanical change.
\end{itemize}
\where{Abstract; Sections III-J, IV (``Human checks''), V-B, X.}

\resp Agreed; this was a real confound (\cnt{athreenlp} of the \cnt{athreeall} A3 studies come from NLP venues, which have no 2026 studies). We now report:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{The ICLR+ICML panel} (Fig.~6, bottom). It has only \cnt{ptwearlyn} studies in 2020--2022, too few to carry a trend from that period. From 2023 to 2026, A2 goes from \cnt{ptwythreeAtwo}\% to \cnt{ptwysixAtwo}\% and A3 from \cnt{ptwythreeAthree}\% to \cnt{ptwysixAthree}\%.
  \item \emph{Venue fixed effects.} A logistic regression on year with venue fixed effects, using all studies, gives an odds ratio per year of \cnt{trAtwo} (\cnt{trAtwolo}--\cnt{trAtwohi}) for A2 and \cnt{trAthree} (\cnt{trAthreelo}--\cnt{trAthreehi}) for A3; A1 shows no consistent trend (\cnt{trAone}, \cnt{trAonelo}--\cnt{trAonehi}).
\end{itemize}
The abstract now states the venue-adjusted result, and the text says that the shift is smaller than the all-venue comparison suggested. \emph{(Question 2.)}

\begin{referee}M4. The growth normalisation answers the wrong question, and its uncertainty is not reported. [\ldots] LM-block denominator; CI for the 14-fold; vocabulary drift.\end{referee}
\resp All three points are addressed, and the first changes our conclusion.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Language-model denominator.} Relative to accepted papers that match the language-model block, the five-venue rate rose from 2020 to 2021 and changed little until 2025: rate ratio 2025 versus 2021--22 of \cnt{rrttlm} (\cnt{rrttlmlo}--\cnt{rrttlmhi}). The ICLR+ICML panel, the only one covering 2026, hints at a recent rise (\cnt{rrtwlm}, \cnt{rrtwlmlo}--\cnt{rrtwlmhi}). The paper now says that the area's growth until 2025 largely tracks the growth of language-model research (Fig.~5, bottom panel; abstract; conclusion).
  \item \emph{Interval.} The 2020--2025 rate ratio is \cnt{rr} with an exact conditional 95\% interval of \cnt{rrlo}--\cnt{rrhi}, based on \cnt{rrkone} studies in 2020; against 2021--22 it is \cnt{rrtt} (\cnt{rrttlo}--\cnt{rrtthi}). The precise multiplier no longer appears without its interval.
  \item \emph{Vocabulary drift.} Counting only studies whose abstracts use language-model vocabulary already common before 2022 gives a rate ratio of \cnt{rrst} (\cnt{rrstlo}--\cnt{rrsthi}), essentially unchanged; \cnt{drifttwentysix}\% of 2026 inclusions still use that vocabulary.
\end{itemize}
\where{Sections III-H, VIII; Fig.~5.}

\begin{referee}M5. Search recall is low, and the query has fixable gaps. [\ldots] Missing terms [\ldots] Re-running an augmented query is cheap.\end{referee}
\resp We ran it (stage~2). It adds every term the referee lists---model checking, Dafny, Verus, Datalog, planner/planning, MILP, linear and integer programming, optimisation models, \emph{proof}, SQL---plus execution traces, TLA+, Alloy and recent model names. It retrieved \cnt{searchtwo} records not screened before; after double screening and adjudication \cnt{searchtwoincl} were included. While doing this we also found that the Paper Copilot lists lacked \cnt{srcbaddanth} main-conference papers at EMNLP 2023/2024 and NAACL 2025; we added them from the ACL Anthology, and applied the track rule consistently to AAAI special tracks (\cnt{srcbaddaaai} papers).

\emph{Question 3, honestly answered.} Recall did not rise. The stage-2 query found real studies, but the enlarged audit (M6) found more missed ones, mostly benchmarks with rule-based generators and agents that act by writing programs, described in vocabulary no query block contains. The estimate for the full search is \cnt{recalltwo} (\cnt{recalltwolo}--\cnt{recalltwohi}), against \cnt{recallmedian} (\cnt{recalllo}--\cnt{recallhi}) for stage~1 under the corrected estimator. The paper now says that it maps roughly half of the eligible literature at these venues, discusses which kinds of studies are missed and in which direction this biases the shares, and reports an independent known-item check (\cnt{kifound} of \cnt{kielig} eligible references of a recent survey found).

\emph{The Prolog remark.} The audit study in question names Prolog only in its full text, not in its abstract; the sentence was misleading and has been rewritten.
\where{Sections III-C, III-D, III-E, IV (``Search recall''), X; Appendix B.}

\begin{referee}M6. The recall point estimate and interval use different estimators. [\ldots] single screener [\ldots] 201 records unexplained.\end{referee}
\resp
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Estimator.} The point estimate is now the posterior median of the same Jeffreys posterior that gives the interval; the plug-in value is shown for reference only. For stage~1 this moves the estimate from 0.60 to \cnt{recallmedian}.
  \item \emph{Second screener.} All \cnt{auditscreened} stage-1 audit records were screened by a second, independent screener ($\kappa=\cnt{kappaaudit}$), and every record either screener did not exclude was adjudicated. We also drew \cnt{auditextn} further audit records (\cnt{audextnein} from the ``neither'' stratum, \cnt{audextlmn} from the language-model-only stratum), double-screened them the same way ($\kappa=\cnt{kappaauditext}$), which narrowed the interval. ``All screening is double'' is now true.
  \item \emph{Accounting (Question 5).} Section III-E now accounts for every Source B record: \cnt{srcbinunion} in the stage-1 union (which contains query hits \emph{and} Source A records that also appear in Source B---these explain the 201), \cnt{srcbsnow} snowball candidates, one duplicate title, one identifier collision, \cnt{srcbaddedtwo} revision additions and \cnt{srcbauditpop} in the audit population, summing to \cnt{sourceb}.
\end{itemize}
\where{Sections III-E, IV (``Search recall''); Table VI.}

\begin{referee}M7. Construct validity: is ``LLM writes Python'' neurosymbolic? [\ldots] Justify R4 more carefully, and report the main findings with and without F3/A1 program-aided studies.\end{referee}
\resp Section III-B now justifies R4: in program-aided reasoning the interpreter computes the answer from a formal artefact the model wrote, the same division of labour as translation into logic for a solver; plain code generation is excluded because the program is the output. We acknowledge that many researchers would draw the line elsewhere and report every main finding without the \cnt{Fthreen} F3 studies (Section V-B): the association (bias-corrected $V=\cnt{woFthreeVc}$) and the A2/A3 trends hold. One finding does change, and we now say so: without program-aided studies, exact checks are the majority within A2 (\cnt{woFthreeAtwoexact} of \cnt{woFthreeAtwo}); the predominance of empirical checking comes almost entirely from program-aided designs.
\where{Sections III-B, V-B, X.}

\begin{referee}M8. The taxonomy mixes two different axes. [\ldots] separate contribution type [\ldots] exact checks rose from 10\% to 29\% combines inference and benchmarks [\ldots] sensitivity under a different order, or record secondary codes.\end{referee}
\resp We did both. Two further coders recorded each study's contribution type (method/benchmark/analysis; $\kappa=\cnt{kappacontrib}$, disagreements adjudicated) and every mechanism it uses ($\kappa=\cnt{mechkappamin}$--\cnt{mechkappamax} per mechanism). This shows that A6 is mostly benchmarks (\cnt{asixbench}) and analyses (\cnt{asixanalysis}), and that \cnt{mechmultipct}\% of method papers combine mechanisms. Re-applying the tree in three other orders changes the 2026 A2 share among methods (\cnt{ordtwosixmin}--\cnt{ordtwosixmax}\%) but not the trends: the venue-adjusted odds ratios are \cnt{ordtwomin}--\cnt{ordtwomax} for A2 and \cnt{ordthreemin}--\cnt{ordthreemax} for A3, all with intervals excluding 1. Exact checking at inference (A2, method papers) is now reported separately from benchmark certification (A6), with its own venue-adjusted trend (odds ratio \cnt{trmexact}, \cnt{trmexactlo}--\cnt{trmexacthi}).
\where{Sections III-F, V, V-B, VIII; Table IV.}

\begin{referee}M9. Coding from abstracts is weak for the attributes that matter most. [\ldots] code check strength and faithfulness from full texts, or move the faithfulness finding out of the abstract.\end{referee}
\resp We did both. An LLM coder recoded check strength and faithfulness from the full text of a seeded random sample of \cnt{ftn} A1/A2 studies (\cnt{ftfull} full texts obtained).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Check strength.} Within A2, full text and abstract agree for \cnt{ftAtwoagree} of \cnt{ftAtwon} studies, so the exact/empirical split is robust. Almost all disagreements are A1 studies where the full-text coder counted the execution that computes the answer as a check; the protocol does not, and we have made that definition explicit. This is very likely also the source of the empirical/none disagreements in the human check.
  \item \emph{Faithfulness.} About half of the formalising studies measure faithfulness somewhere in the paper (\cnt{ftfaithyes} of \cnt{ftfaithrel}), but only \cnt{ftfaithabs} say so in the abstract. Our earlier claim that faithfulness is ``rarely reported'' was an artefact of abstract-level coding. The finding is out of the abstract, rewritten in Section VII as ``often unmeasured and rarely foregrounded'', and challenge C1 is revised accordingly.
\end{itemize}
\where{Sections III-F, VII, IX, X.}

  \item \textbf{``All screening is double''.} Now true (M6).
  \item \textbf{11 vs 9 benchmarks.} Table X now lists every benchmark named by five or more studies (\cnt{benchfiveplus} after the revision).
  \item \textbf{miniF2F mentions.} Confirmed: every included abstract that mentions miniF2F is a D1 study; the text now says so.
  \item \textbf{$10^5$, $10^4$.} These are typeset as powers in the PDF; the text-extraction artefact does not affect the paper.
  \item \textbf{Fig.~5 legend.} Series are labelled with their venue sets; the figure now has two panels (all papers, language-model papers).
  \item \textbf{Track policy and Findings.} Section III-B states the rule (every peer-reviewed research track; AAAI special tracks added for consistency with IJCAI; survey tracks removed by E5) and explains the exclusion of ACL Findings, naming Logic-LM as a cost.
  \item \textbf{E2 vs A7.} R2 now states that a frozen (V)LM that emits the symbols a symbolic core reasons over is A7 and included; E2 applies to feature extractors, background and baselines.
  \item \textbf{Table X.} ``Prover fine-tunes'' is labelled as a role; code-specialised models (Codex, StarCoder, CodeGen) are separated from GPT-3.5/ChatGPT; the caption states that counts are unions and upper bounds.
  \item \textbf{Snowballing.} Section III-E states that backward snowballing covered only studies with local full text and that we did not snowball forward; instead of extending it, we measured what all routes miss with the enlarged audit and the known-item check.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{LLM-as-reviewer methodology (\S5)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Agreement statistics.} Table VI adds $\kappa$ without records both screeners marked unsure (stage 1: \cnt{kappanuunion}); Section III-I says that agreement between same-family models mainly measures rule determinism.
  \item \textbf{Unmeasured adjudication.} Section III-I reports that \cnt{singleadjincl} inclusions (\cnt{singleadjinclpct}\%) rest on a single adjudicator; the human check reports precision separately for adjudicated and agreed inclusions.
  \item \textbf{Reproducibility.} Reworded to ``reproducible analysis'': the pipeline recomputes every number from the decision files; the decisions are auditable but not regenerable.
  \item \textbf{Assistant-made decisions.} Reversing the assistant's audit decision changes recall from \cnt{recalltwo} to \cnt{recallassist}; its coding decision moves one study between A2 and A3.
  \item \textbf{Prior work.} Section II-C now cites evaluations of LLM screening beyond the single study (Guo et al.\ 2024; Tran et al.\ 2024; Dennst\"adt et al.\ 2024), and notes that the evidence comes mainly from biomedicine.
\end{itemize}
